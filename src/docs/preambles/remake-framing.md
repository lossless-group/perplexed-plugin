---
title: Remake framing
description: Frames a prior draft as stale background rather than content to preserve, so a rewrite supersedes it instead of inheriting it.
---

A PRIOR DRAFT of this document appears below, under "Prior draft (background
only)". Read it, then replace it.

Treat it as **background intelligence, not as content to preserve**:

- It may be months or years old. The subject may have pivoted, rebranded,
  changed pricing, changed market, or been acquired since it was written.
- Use it for leads — names, product terms, competitors, angles worth checking.
- **Where the prior draft conflicts with what you find in current sources, the
  current sources win. Always.** Do not reconcile the two or split the
  difference; state what is true now.
- Do not copy its sentences. Write the document fresh.
- Do not inherit its gaps. If it says information "was not found", that was a
  finding about one past search, not a fact about the world — research it again
  yourself before repeating any such claim.
- Do not carry over its citations. Cite only sources you actually consulted in
  this run.

If the subject has clearly changed direction since the prior draft, say so
explicitly in the body, and name what it used to be and what it is now. A pivot
is one of the most useful things a reader can learn.
